Responsible Gaming: You're Not Alone. Here's Help.

If Jai Club (or any gambling) has started affecting your life — financially, emotionally, or in your relationships — please read this. We promise no judgement. Just resources.

📅 Updated May 2026⏱ 7 min read
If you need to talk right now:

Free, confidential support services are available in India through organizations like Vandrevala Foundation, NIMHANS Bengaluru, and AASRA. Visit their websites for contact details and support options.

All free. All confidential. They will not judge you.

1. Signs that gambling has become a problem

You don't need to be losing crores to have a gambling problem. Warning signs (any one is meaningful, multiple is serious):

  • You play more often or for longer than you intend
  • You've borrowed money to play (from friends, family, credit cards, instant-loan apps)
  • You hide play from family or partner
  • You play to "win back" previous losses
  • You feel restless or anxious when you can't play
  • You've missed work, classes or appointments because of play
  • You've lied about how much you've spent or won
  • You've used money meant for rent, EMIs, school fees or family needs
  • You feel guilt, shame or regret after play but go back anyway

2. What problem gambling does to you

This isn't moral judgement — it's neuroscience. Gambling triggers dopamine in the same brain pathways as cocaine. After repeated exposure, the brain adapts: you need more to feel the same, you crave it during withdrawal, and you struggle with normal pleasures. This is real. It is not a "lack of willpower." It is a measurable brain change. And it is treatable.

3. First steps you can take today

Block your access (immediately)

  • Delete the Jai Club app from your phone
  • Use BetBlocker (free) or Gamban (paid) to block gambling sites/apps at the device level
  • Ask your bank to flag/block payments to gambling-related merchants (most major Indian banks support this)
  • Lower your UPI daily limit to ₹500 or ₹1,000

Talk to someone

Pick the lowest-friction option. It can be:

  • A free confidential support service (see organizations listed at the top of this page)
  • A trusted family member or friend
  • Your doctor or family physician
  • A counsellor via apps like Wysa, YourDost or Lyf Smile (some offer free trials)

Address the financial damage

  • Make a list of debts and to whom — total amount
  • Pause new spending. Use cash only for a month if possible
  • If debt is to instant-loan apps, know that the RBI guidance protects you from harassment — file complaint with RBI Sachet (sachet.rbi.org.in)
  • Negotiate with creditors honestly — most are surprisingly accommodating when contacted before default

4. Self-exclusion options

Jai Club itself does not offer a robust self-exclusion option (this is a critical regulatory failure of grey-market apps). But you can self-exclude at the device and payment levels:

  • Device: BetBlocker (Android, iOS) is free and effective
  • UPI: set daily UPI transaction limit to ₹0 in your bank app, only raising for known purposes
  • Cards: ask your bank to disable "international/online merchant" transactions
  • Trusted accountability partner: share screen-time / location / spending reports with someone who cares about you

5. If a family member is struggling

If someone you love is in trouble:

  • Approach with concern, not judgement
  • Don't bail them out financially (it usually accelerates the cycle)
  • Encourage professional help — offer to make the call with them
  • Take care of yourself too — Al-Anon-style support groups exist for families of problem gamblers

6. A note on this site

We document Jai Club because pretending the apps don't exist doesn't help anyone. Banning Telegram channels and YouTube videos hasn't stopped users from arriving. Our hope: better information, fewer surprises, more people walking away with their finances intact.

You can recover.

People recover from gambling problems every day — financially, emotionally, in relationships. It takes work, but it's entirely possible. The first step — reaching out for help — is the hardest. You don't have to do this alone.
