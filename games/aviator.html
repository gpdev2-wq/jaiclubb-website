1. What is Aviator?

Aviator is a "crash" multiplier game — one of the most viral gambling-game formats of the past 5 years globally, originating from Spribe and cloned across hundreds of grey-market platforms including Jai Club. A round consists of a virtual plane taking off, with a multiplier (1×, 1.5×, 2×, 3×, 10×, sometimes 100×+) increasing in real time as the plane flies. Players place a bet before takeoff and must "cash out" before the plane "crashes" (flies away). Cash out late = lose everything. Cash out early = lock in that multiplier × your bet.

The genius (and the danger) of Aviator is that you control when to exit — making it feel like skill. It is not. The crash point is generated by the server RNG at the moment the round starts, before you can do anything.

2. How a round works

  1. Pre-round phase (~5 seconds): place your bet (single or split into two).
  2. Plane takes off; multiplier starts at 1.00× and rises.
  3. You can cash out at any time to lock in the current multiplier × bet.
  4. At a server-determined random time, the plane "flies away" — anyone still in the round loses their bet.
  5. Round ends; next begins in ~5 seconds.

A single round lasts anywhere from 1 second (instant crash) to 30+ seconds (high multiplier). Rounds per hour: 180+. Highest cycle speed of any Jai Club game.

The advertised "RTP" (return to player) on Aviator is typically 97% — meaning 3% house edge. But in practice the effective edge is higher because of cash-out behaviour: most players panic-cash too early on big multipliers and ride too long on small ones, both of which hurt expected value.

4. Does auto-cashout help?

Aviator lets you pre-set an auto-cashout multiplier (e.g., "auto cash out at 1.5×"). Many players believe this makes the game "safe." It doesn't.

If you auto-cash at 1.5×, you win when the crash is ≥1.5× (which we observed in 65% of rounds) and lose when crash < 1.5× (35%). Per-round expectation:

  • Win: 65% × 0.5× bet = +0.325 bet
  • Lose: 35% × bet = −0.35 bet
  • Net: −0.025 bet → ~2.5% house edge

Auto-cashout doesn't eliminate the edge; it just locks in a more predictable but still negative expectation. You will lose money on auto-cashout too, just more slowly.

Martingale warning:

The "double after a loss" Aviator strategy is mathematically broken. After 8 consecutive losses (which happens — we saw streaks of 9 in our data), you'd need to bet 256× your starting bet to recover. Either your bankroll is exhausted, or the platform's max bet caps you out. Either way: catastrophic loss.

6. Our 500-round results

Combining all our Aviator sessions (₹50–₹200 bets, varied strategies): total turnover ~₹52,000, net loss ~₹2,800, effective house edge ~5.4%. Substantially higher than the advertised 3% because of human cash-out behaviour bias.

7. The "Aviator prediction channel" scam

Telegram channels claim to "predict next Aviator multiplier" using "AI" or "hacked algorithms." All fake. The multiplier is generated server-side at round start. No external observer can predict it. Channels make money from:

  • Paid subscriptions for "VIP signals"
  • Referral commissions when you sign up to Jai Club through their link
  • Selectively highlighting wins and deleting losses
