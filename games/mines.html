Jai Club Mines Review: Why "Strategy" Doesn't Save You.

1. What is Mines?

Mines is a tile-reveal game inspired by classic Windows Minesweeper. A 5×5 grid (25 tiles) is shown. The player chooses how many bombs are hidden (anywhere from 1 to 24) before placing a bet. Each safe tile flipped increases the multiplier. The player can cash out at any time. Hit a bomb = lose entire bet.

2. How a round works

  1. Choose number of bombs (1 to 24). More bombs = higher multipliers per safe flip, but lower survival probability.
  2. Place bet (₹10 minimum).
  3. Click "Start." The grid is fixed — bomb positions are randomly chosen at this point.
  4. Click tiles one at a time. Each safe reveal increases the multiplier.
  5. Cash out any time = win (multiplier × bet) − bet.
  6. Hit a bomb = lose entire bet.

3. The probability math

This is the section the game wishes you wouldn't read. The math is exact:

If the grid has N tiles and M bombs, the probability that your first random tile is safe is (N−M)/N. The probability of two consecutive safe tiles is (N−M)/N × (N−M−1)/(N−1). And so on.

The multiplier the game pays is calibrated to be slightly less than the fair odds — that's how the house edge gets in. House edge across all bomb counts on Jai Club's Mines is approximately 3% to 5% (per round, regardless of how many tiles you flip).

The 20-bomb option is the riskiest: a single click pays ~5× but has only 20% survival probability — expected value ~5 × 0.20 = ~1.0, with the house edge subtracted leaves you at ~95% return. Identical structure to the other bomb counts.

5. The "skill illusion"

Here's the trap: choosing which tile to click feels like a decision. It is not. Bomb positions are randomly determined at round start and don't depend on which tile you pick. Every untouched tile has exactly the same probability of being a bomb. Clicking the corner or the centre is mathematically identical. "Pattern" claims on YouTube (e.g., "the bomb is always in column 3") are statistical nonsense — there is no pattern because there's no per-game seed visible to the player.

The "skill" element exists only in cash-out timing: when do you stop? This is real but it's also the same decision as Aviator (cash out at 2×? 5×? 10×?). And the math says: every additional safe flip adds the same house edge to your expected return. You can't out-strategy the rake.
